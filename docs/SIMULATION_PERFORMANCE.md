# CPU simulation optimization
Goal: reduce repeated NPC streaming queries and traffic prediction allocations without reducing simulation frequency or density.
Authorities: EntityStreamSystem, NpcSystem spatial rebuild, TrafficDriverRuntime/TrafficJourneyPlanner.
Acceptance: immediate alert/chunk changes outside batches; same decisions inside a batch; same route projections; traffic and streaming tests and a before/after browser sample.
Non-goals: lower NPC/vehicle population, movement/physics throttling, alternate update loop, art changes.

## Implemented (2026-09-18)
- Batch NPC chunk lookups within each synchronous collection only; keep alert and later chunk changes immediate.
- Avoid full driver copies in prediction and reject clearly worse route segments before distance calculation.
- Cache immutable pedestrian patrol routes and zone membership; officer movement/progress remains per-frame.
- Preserve traffic density and simulation frequency.

## Follow-up: vehicle visibility and traffic CPU
Goal: skip off-camera vehicle drawing and reduce redundant traffic geometry queries while preserving motion.
In scope: VehicleView shared vehicle composition, a camera visibility helper, TrafficDriverWorld neighbor candidates, TrafficLocalBehaviorSystem compiled lane geometry and obstacle projections shared within its decision batch, focused regressions. The existing traffic runtime and shared vehicle kinematics remain authoritative.
Out of scope: traffic density, timestep, collision frequency, route rules, city geometry, art quality, publication or a new update loop.
Acceptance: off-camera containers skip their child drawing; viewport-edge parts remain visible under zoom/rotation/scale; visible/active state and simulation stay unchanged; optimized traffic returns the same decisions and geometry; browser timing and visual comparison plus native traffic coverage.
Validation: affected plan and execution, focused culling/performance/driver/recovery/transit checks, production build, movement sample and paired frozen rendering comparison. Delivery remains local on port 4174.

Implemented: vehicle containers use a conservative local radius measured once from all visual parts (refreshed on pool repaint); willRender skips child submission outside the camera without changing active/visible flags used by gameplay. Rotated cameras, nested containers and nonstandard scroll factors retain the original path. TrafficDriverWorld retains its spatial membership between steps and caches at most 64 ordered candidate regions, invalidating on cell, membership or order changes; every query still checks live coordinates/state and current streamed buildings. Local behavior compiles fixed lane segment lengths/directions on lane-cache rebuild and shares obstacle projections only within synchronous decision collection, with immediate position-change checks and cleanup before decisions are applied.
Evidence: alternating isolated neighbor-query benchmark, 76,800 queries/run, original 577–666 ms vs cached 169–201 ms, identical checksums. Native 600-step replay with 606 traffic identities (including six buses) produces identical full-agent hashes at every one-second checkpoint. Separate full replay timing did not demonstrate a gain (route mean 5.36 vs 6.91 ms); do not confuse the isolated improvement with total FPS.
Frozen visual comparison: 1400x850 screenshots with vehicle culling enabled/disabled are pixel-identical at zoom 0.9 and 1.4. At 0.9, 46 of 47 active vehicles are submitted (975 vs 998 child parts); at 1.4, 27 of 47 (574 vs 998 parts). No missing edge geometry observed. Paired render CPU timings were noisy and do not establish a precise culling speedup.

No page errors. State and workload evolve separately; this is indicative, not a deterministic FPS guarantee. Compiled lane geometry was added after this sample and is measured separately below.
