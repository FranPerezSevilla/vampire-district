# ViceBlood main-theme source provenance

## Composition

- **Work:** *Gnossienne No. 1*
- **Composer:** Erik Satie (1866–1925)
- **Composition date shown in the score:** 1890
- **Status:** public-domain composition

## Source used for this MIDI rebuild

This MIDI was independently transcribed from the **1913 Rouart, Lerolle & Cie.**
printed score held by the Bibliothèque nationale de France:

- BnF shelfmark: `FOL-VM12-3932 (1)`
- Gallica identifier: `btv1b20000137m`
- Publisher: Rouart, Lerolle & Cie., Paris
- Printed plate for No. 1: `R.L. 9884 & Cie.`
- Score pages used: image 4/8 (opening) and image 5/8 (continuation/final)

Public-domain scan mirrors used for visual transcription:

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trois_Gnossiennes,_pour_piano_-_btv1b20000137m_(4_of_8).jpg
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trois_Gnossiennes,_pour_piano_-_btv1b20000137m_(5_of_8).jpg

The Wikimedia Commons file pages identify the 1913 work/scan as public domain.
The rebuild does **not** use the Mutopia LilyPond transcription and therefore
does not carry the CC BY-SA dependency that applied to the earlier discarded
prototype MIDI.

## What is original Satie material vs. ViceBlood material

The MIDI keeps these tracks separate:

1. `Satie - Melody`
2. `Satie - Harmony`
3. `Satie - Bass`
4. `ViceBlood - Night Drone`
5. `ViceBlood - Heartbeat / Metal`
6. `ViceBlood - Neon Texture`

Tracks 1–3 are the score transcription. Tracks 4–6 are the ViceBlood arrangement
layers and can be replaced or edited freely in the DAW.

Grace notes are realized as short MIDI acciaccaturas for playback/editing. This
is a performance realization choice, not a change to the printed pitches.
